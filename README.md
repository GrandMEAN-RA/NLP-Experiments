# NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING (NLP) EXPERIMENTS

These experiments provide clean comparative study across three axes: predictive performance, computational cost (training), and deployment 
efficiency (inference), under two distinct hardware regimes (Low-resource, High-resource). The results are particularly informative for 
resource-constrained NLP in low-resource languages (Hausa).

**Project Relevance:** 
The projects directly demonstrate:
1. How much additional predictive performance do transformer models provide relative to their computational cost?
2. Which modelling approach is most appropriate when computational resources are limited?
3. How consistently do different modelling approaches perform, and what computational costs accompany their predictive behaviour?
4. How should practitioners choose between competing NLP models when accuracy, latency and computational resources impose different constraints?

## EXPERIMENT 1:
### Predictive Performance and Computational Efficiency of Classical and Transformer-Based Models for Low-Resource Language Sentiment Analysis: 
### An Empirical Study of Hausa.

**Goal:** Compare classical machine learning (Logistic Regression + TF-IDF) against a multilingual transformer (mBERT) and its
light-weight version, DistilBERT, for Hausa sentiment classification under low-resource conditions.
